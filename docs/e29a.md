## Method and integrity checks

- Reused the exact 89-checkpoint E22-B roster, five saved E13-B outer folds, E22-B classifiers, E25 budget models and each E25 fold's saved roster.
- Verified V0 IDs, labels, text and prompt IDs against every `v0_group.original_id`; each scenario has 500 unique source responses, all 18 labels, all group sizes 1–18, and true groups with one original prompt and no repeated label.
- Reproduced E22-B's saved OOF scores with its five saved fold models. Reproduced every E25 budget score matrix from its saved fold classifier and fold-local roster. The resulting full-V0 held-out predictions match the saved artifacts.
- Recomputed each of the 89 single-checkpoint removals and the three existing near-duplicate-cluster removals from cached checkpoint probabilities using the saved E22-B fold models. Reduced score matrices are cached under `artifacts/analysis/e29a/score_matrices/`.
- For every scenario and roster, independently decoded top-1, true `group_id` Hungarian, and frozen E19-D group Hungarian. The frozen E19-D IDs were restricted to the current scenario and namespaced by their original held-out fold. This is a frozen-group subset diagnostic, **not fresh grouping**.
- No neural inference, CatBoost fitting, threshold selection, or roster selection was performed.

The 25,000 scenario rows map to all 2,699 V0 IDs. Each scenario independently contains all 18 labels. The scenario files, fold assignments, label metrics, predictions and row-level scenario results are in `artifacts/analysis/e29a/`.

## Stability of E25 pruning decisions

E25's strongest checkpoint ranking largely holds under incomplete prompt groups when the final decoder uses Hungarian assignment. Spearman correlation between the original E25 importance rank and mean scenario removal rank is **0.784** for true-group Hungarian and **0.777** for frozen E19-D Hungarian. It falls to **0.430** for independent classification, so importance depends more on the downstream assignment method when no group constraint is used.

| Checkpoint | Original E25 rank | Mean rank, frozen E19-D | Mean BA loss when removed | 10th–90th percentile loss | Scenarios removal helped |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| N0048 | 1 | 1.74 | 4.82 pp | 2.56 to 7.60 pp | 0% |
| N0030 | 2 | 2.08 | 4.13 pp | 2.13 to 6.31 pp | 0% |
| N0093 | 3 | 3.92 | 3.74 pp | 1.16 to 6.29 pp | 2% |
| N0107 | 4 | 14.32 | 1.52 pp | 0.14 to 3.01 pp | 8% |
| N0009 | 6 | 19.96 | 1.18 pp | −0.06 to 2.36 pp | 12% |
| N0090 | 9 | 22.98 | 1.02 pp | −0.19 to 2.36 pp | 16% |
| N0105 | 5 | 23.18 | 0.91 pp | 0.04 to 1.97 pp | 10% |

N0048, N0030 and N0093 are the most consistent contributors across scenario compositions and all three decoding methods. N0107 is also consistently helpful, though less dominant. These are contribution observations, not a new deployment selection.

Some weaker checkpoints' effects vary substantially with group size. By frozen E19-D assignment, N0093's mean removal loss ranges by **7.54 pp** across true group sizes; N0048 varies by 6.72 pp and N0105 by 6.32 pp. The full checkpoint-by-size breakdown is `checkpoint_group_size_stability.csv`. These variations argue against treating one full-V0 marginal rank as a universal checkpoint value.

## Hungarian and roster size

Hungarian adds little for small groups and more for large groups. Comparing row-weighted accuracy across true group-size bands:

| E22-B, 89 | 5–9 | 77.38% | 80.34% (+2.96 pp) | 80.19% (+2.81 pp) |
| E22-B, 89 | 10–18 | 77.70% | 85.72% (+8.02 pp) | 85.19% (+7.49 pp) |
| E25-60 | 1–4 | 71.84% | 72.93% (+1.09 pp) | 72.93% (+1.09 pp) |

| E25-60 | 10–18 | 69.43% | 78.02% (+8.58 pp) | 77.53% (+8.09 pp) |
| E25-20 | 1–4 | 66.02% | 67.11% (+1.09 pp) | 67.11% (+1.09 pp) |
| E25-20 | 5–9 | 63.56% | 66.84% (+3.29 pp) | 66.80% (+3.25 pp) |

Singleton groups are unchanged because assignment has no competing responses. For the 89-checkpoint baseline, the independent-to-Hungarian gain grows from about 1 pp for group sizes 1–4 to 7.5–8 pp for sizes 10–18. Pruning preserves that pattern, but on a lower score level.

E25-60 is the strongest and most practically stable of the existing reduced rosters: it leads the reduced budgets on mean, 10th-percentile and minimum balanced accuracy under both Hungarian decoders. E25-40 has the lowest scenario standard deviation among reduced rosters for the Hungarian methods (about 2.18–2.20 pp), but its mean is about 5.35 pp below E25-60 under true-group assignment. None of the five reduced budgets beats the 89-checkpoint baseline in mean balanced accuracy; each loses in all 50 scenarios for every decoder.

## Findings

1. **Does E25's checkpoint ranking remain stable on `v0_group`?** Mostly for Hungarian use: Spearman 0.78 against the original E25 ranking, with the same top three. Stability is materially weaker for independent prediction (0.43).
2. **Which checkpoints consistently provide useful complementary predictions?** N0048, N0030 and N0093 are the strongest; N0107 is a second tier. Their removals reduce balanced accuracy in 92–100% of scenarios under frozen E19-D assignment.
3. **How does pruning affect Hungarian accuracy for small versus large groups?** Hungarian provides roughly 1 pp for sizes 1–4, 3 pp for sizes 5–9, and 7–9 pp for sizes 10–18. The constraint helps most when many labels compete in the same group.
4. **Which existing roster budgets have the most stable performance?** E25-60 has the best accuracy and worst-case accuracy among reduced rosters. E25-40 has slightly lower scenario variance, but much worse mean and lower-tail scores. The 20–30 budgets are not attractive on this benchmark.
5. **Does this justify a different checkpoint-selection strategy?** Yes, as a follow-up to test on external grouped data. The weaker rank correlation for independent prediction and checkpoint-by-group-size variation suggest selection should account for runtime and expected group-size distribution, not only full-V0 marginal contribution. This analysis does not select a replacement roster because `v0_group` reuses V0.

E25 is the E22-B-based ensemble study. Its results are separate from the deployed E13-B v037 Kaggle router. The experiment catalog records E29-A as a V0-reuse robustness analysis, not as a new submission candidate.
